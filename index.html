How I work

Ship it, then improve it

A thing on a server that five people are using beats a perfect thing on my laptop. Every project here went live before it was finished, and got better because people were using it.

Small stack on purpose

Flask, SQLite, plain CSS. No build step where one is not needed. It means a client can be handed the whole thing and it will still run in three years.

Security is not an add-on

I study for the CEH and PenTest+ because I would rather find the broken access check in my own code than have someone else find it. I have, more than once.

About

I came to code sideways, and that turns out to be the useful part.

I am Dutch, based in Auckland, and I work in stock control and purchasing — the job that taught me what a broken process actually costs a business. Before that I spent a year as an AI trainer, which is where I learned to read a system carefully enough to explain why it is wrong.

Now I build the software. Mostly small web apps for a specific group of people with a specific problem: a pool for a group of friends, a study tool for an exam I am sitting, a sweepstake product for clubs that currently run theirs on a spreadsheet. I am studying the CEH and working toward CompTIA Security+ and PenTest+, aiming at automation work where security and AI meet.

What that means if you are hiring or looking for someone to build something: I finish, I deploy, and I will tell you plainly what a thing costs and what it cannot do.
